# Are Groceries Really Cheaper in the U.S.?
# A Canada-U.S. Food Afoordability Analysis

#Project Overview
This project anayzes whether groceries are more affordable in the United States or Canada when accounting for grocery prices, inflation, wages, and purchasing power.

Using open data from Stats Canada, the U.S. Bureau of Labour statistics, FRED and other public economic data sources, this project compares grocery affordability between Canada and the United States from 2017 to 2025.

The main goal is to measure grocery affordability using wage-adjusted metrices, especially: 
- Grocery price inflation over time
- Grocery basket costs
- Average weekly earnings
- Estimated hours of work needed to buy a representative grocery basket
- Grocery costs as a share of weekly earnings
  
A secondary analysis examines whether changes in Canadian farm products prices help explain changes in Canadian retail grocery prices with a time lag.

## Research Questions
This project investigates the following questions:
1. Have grocery prices increased faster in Canada or the United States?
2. Are groceries more affordable in the U.S. than in Canada when adjusted for wages and purchasing power?
3. How many hours of work are required to pruchase a representative grocery basket in each country?
4. Has grocery affordability improved or worsened over time?
5. Do Canadian farm product prices show evidence of passing through to retail grocery prices with a lag?
